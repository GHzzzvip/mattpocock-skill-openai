# Shape an article

Read the supplied material in full and preserve it as source. Use a separate article destination. Establish audience, intended argument, and what the reader already knows, reusing context already given.

Offer a few materially different openings when the angle remains undecided. Develop the chosen direction one useful block at a time, checking what the reader needs next. Introduce concepts before relying on them. Choose prose, a list, a table, a quotation, or code because it serves the content, not to satisfy a format quota.

Discuss consequential structural choices; do not force approval of every routine sentence if the user asks for an autonomous draft. In collaborative mode, append agreed blocks immediately. Re-read before editing to preserve user changes.

Identify gaps in the source rather than inventing evidence. A fragment may be split, recombined, or omitted. Keep the voice consistent and revise transitions so the article fulfills its opening promise.

Done when the requested draft or collaborative section is complete. Report the article path and unresolved evidence needs. Publishing and modifying the source material are outside this drafting task unless requested.
