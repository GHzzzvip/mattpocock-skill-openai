# Draft a journey of beats

Read the raw material and establish the audience's prior knowledge and the article destination. A beat makes one narrative move: establish a scene, land a point, introduce an idea, or shift perspective. Its length follows that purpose.

Offer a few distinct reachable starting beats. Explain what each introduces and where it could lead. After the user chooses, write that beat, reread the article, and offer useful alternatives for the next move. Track concepts introduced so later beats have the foundations they require.

Preserve source material and user edits. Rewrite a selected beat when requested without replacing unrelated sections. Use supplied facts faithfully and flag missing evidence. If the user requests autonomous drafting, follow that scope rather than imposing turn-by-turn approval.

End when the narrative answers its central promise, even if source fragments remain unused. Report the saved article and any unfinished questions; do not publish it without a publishing request.
